# Example Rubrics Aligned with Core Values
## Student-Centered, Kind, and Precise Assessment Tools

---

## 💙 Philosophy: What Makes a "Kind" Rubric?

**Traditional rubrics often:**
- Use deficit language ("lacks," "poor," "inadequate")
- Create anxiety ("Did I hit every point?")
- Feel like judgment rather than feedback
- Are overly complex or vague

**Values-aligned rubrics:**
- Use growth language ("developing," "approaching," "mastered")
- Provide clear path forward
- Feel like a roadmap, not a verdict
- Are specific enough to be helpful, simple enough to be usable

## 📊 Example 1: Writing Assignment Rubric (Elementary/Middle School)

### Assignment: Persuasive Paragraph

**Learning Goal:** Write a paragraph that clearly states an opinion and supports it with reasons and examples.

---

| Criterion | Still Building 🌱 | Making Progress 🌿 | Got It! 🌟 | Above & Beyond 🚀 |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|------------|------------------|
| **Clear Opinion Statement** | I can tell you have an idea, but I'm not sure what you're arguing for. Let's make it clearer! | You state an opinion! To make it stronger, put it right at the beginning. | Your opinion is clear and right at the start. Perfect! | Your opinion is clear AND tells me why this topic matters. Excellent hook! |
| **Supporting Reasons** | You have one reason. Let's add 2 more to make your argument stronger! | You have 2-3 reasons. Great! Some need more explanation. | You have 3 solid reasons that support your opinion clearly. | You have 3+ strong reasons AND you explained why each one matters. |
| **Examples/Evidence** | I'd love to see specific examples! Can you add details or facts to support your ideas? | You have some examples. Add more specific details to make them stronger! | Each reason has a specific example. Well done! | Your examples are specific, detailed, and really persuasive! |
| **Organization** | Your ideas are all here! Let's organize them in an order that makes sense. | Your paragraph has a beginning and end. Let's work on making the middle flow better. | Your paragraph is well-organized: opinion → reasons → conclusion. | Your paragraph flows beautifully with clear transitions between ideas! |
| **Conclusion** | Your paragraph ends, but let's add a sentence that wraps up your argument. | You have a conclusion! Let's make sure it restates your main opinion. | Your conclusion restates your opinion clearly. Nice work! | Your conclusion restates your opinion AND makes me think more about the topic! |

---

### How to Use This Rubric:

**Before Assignment:**
Show students the rubric. Explain: "This shows you what I'm looking for! Use it like a checklist."

**During Work:**
Students can self-check: "Which column am I in? What do I need to do to move forward?"

**After Grading:**
Circle or highlight where student is. Write 1-2 specific next steps.

**Example Feedback:**
> **Your work:** Making Progress 🌿 on most criteria, Got It! 🌟 on clear opinion!
>
> **What's working well:** Your opinion statement is crystal clear! I know exactly what you think.
>
> **Next steps to grow:**
> 1. Add one more reason to support your opinion (you have 2, shoot for 3!)
> 2. For your example about recess, add a specific detail (what exactly happened?)
>
> **You're learning! Keep going!** 💙

## 📊 Example 2: Math Problem-Solving Rubric (All Grades)

### Assignment: Multi-Step Math Problems

**Learning Goal:** Solve multi-step problems accurately and explain your thinking clearly.

---

| Criterion | Still Learning 🟡 | Almost There 🟢 | Mastered! ⭐ |
|-----------|------------------|-----------------|------------|
| **Correct Answer** | Not quite yet! But I can see what you were thinking. Let's figure out where to adjust. | You're so close! Small error—check your work at [step X]. | Correct answer! Well done! |
| **Work Shown** | I need to see your steps to help you! Show me how you got your answer. | Most steps are there. I'd love to see [specific step] shown more clearly. | All steps shown clearly. I can follow your thinking! |
| **Strategy/Method** | Let's talk about which strategy might work best for this problem. | You used a strategy! Let's make sure it's the right fit for this problem type. | You chose an effective strategy and used it correctly! |
| **Explanation** | I'd love to hear your thinking! Can you explain in words how you solved it? | You explained some of your thinking. Can you tell me more about [specific part]? | Your explanation is clear! I understand exactly how you thought through this. |
| **Check Your Work** | Did you check your answer? Let's talk about ways to verify. | You checked! [Specific feedback about checking method] | You checked AND showed how you know your answer is right! |

---

### Kind Language Note:

❌ **Don't say:** "Incorrect. -5 points."

✅ **Do say:** "Not quite! But I see you [what they did right]. The error is at [specific place]. Try [specific strategy] and let's see if you get it!"

## 📊 Example 3: Science Lab Report (Middle/High School)

### Assignment: Experiment Write-Up

**Learning Goal:** Document an experiment following scientific method and draw valid conclusions.

---

| Component | Needs Revision | Proficient | Advanced |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|----------|
| **Hypothesis** | You made a prediction! Let's make it more specific and testable. Format: "If [I do this], then [this will happen] because [reason]." | Your hypothesis is clear and testable. It makes a specific prediction. Well done! | Your hypothesis is testable, specific, AND based on scientific reasoning. You explained why you think this will happen! |
| **Procedure** | You described what you did. To strengthen it, add enough detail that someone else could repeat your experiment exactly. | Your procedure is detailed and clear. Someone could follow these steps! | Your procedure is thorough, includes safety notes, and explains WHY each step is important. Excellent! |
| **Data Collection** | You have some data. Let's organize it in a table or chart to make it easier to see patterns. | Your data is organized clearly in a table or graph. Great! | Your data is organized, labeled clearly, and you noted any observations or surprises. Very thorough! |
| **Analysis** | You noticed what happened. Now let's dig deeper: What patterns do you see? What might explain them? | You analyzed your data and identified patterns. Good scientific thinking! | You deeply analyzed your data, identified patterns, AND discussed possible sources of error or variation. Excellent critical thinking! |
| **Conclusion** | You stated results. Also answer: Did your results support your hypothesis? Why or why not? What would you do differently? | Your conclusion answers the key questions and connects back to your hypothesis. Well done! | Your conclusion is thorough, discusses what you learned, suggests next questions to investigate, and shows deep scientific thinking! |

---

### How to Give Feedback:

**For "Needs Revision":**
- Point to specific section
- Explain what's missing or unclear
- Give specific next step or example
- Offer to help or review revision

**Example:**
> **Hypothesis section:** You made a prediction—that's the start! Let's make it more specific. Instead of "Plants will grow," try "If I give plants 8 hours of sunlight per day, then they will grow taller than plants with only 4 hours because sunlight is needed for photosynthesis." See how that's more detailed and explains your reasoning? Give that format a try and come show me!

## 📊 Example 4: Participation Rubric (Self-Assessment)

### Assignment: Class Discussion Participation

**This is for student SELF-assessment—they reflect on their own participation.**

---

### 💭 My Participation This Week:

**Choose the statement that best describes you. Be honest—this helps you grow!**

#### Listening & Respect
- ⬜ I'm working on this: I sometimes got distracted or talked while others were speaking
- ⬜ I'm doing well: I listened when others spoke and showed respect
- ⬜ I'm excelling: I listened actively, made eye contact, and built on others' ideas

**My goal for next week:** ___________________________________________

#### Contributing Ideas
- ⬜ I'm working on this: I didn't share my ideas this week (it's okay! let's work on it)
- ⬜ I'm doing well: I shared my ideas at least once
- ⬜ I'm excelling: I shared multiple times and helped others share too

**My goal for next week:** ___________________________________________

#### Quality of Contributions
- ⬜ I'm working on this: I spoke, but need to think more deeply about the topic
- ⬜ I'm doing well: My comments showed I was thinking about the topic
- ⬜ I'm excelling: My comments showed deep thinking, asked good questions, or connected ideas

**My goal for next week:** ___________________________________________

#### Helping Others
- ⬜ I'm working on this: I mostly focused on my own ideas
- ⬜ I'm doing well: I responded to others' ideas respectfully
- ⬜ I'm excelling: I asked questions to help others clarify, or encouraged quiet students to share

**My goal for next week:** ___________________________________________

---

### Teacher Note:
This self-assessment puts students in the driver's seat. They reflect honestly, set goals, and take ownership.

Your role:
- Conference briefly with students about their self-assessment
- Help them set realistic goals
- Celebrate growth, not just achievement
- If student self-assessment doesn't match your observations, have a conversation

## 📊 Example 5: Project Rubric with Student Choice

### Assignment: Social Studies Research Project

**Students choose how to demonstrate learning:**
- Option A: Written report
- Option B: Presentation (slides, poster, or oral)
- Option C: Creative project (video, model, artwork + explanation)

**All options assessed on SAME criteria—just different formats!**

---

| Criterion | What This Means | Developing | Proficient | Advanced |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Content Knowledge** | Do you show deep understanding of the topic? | Surface-level information; mostly facts without explanation | Solid understanding; you explain concepts clearly | Deep understanding; you make connections and analyze |
| **Research Quality** | Did you use credible sources and cite them? | 1-2 sources; may not be credible; missing citations | 3+ credible sources; citations included | 5+ credible sources; variety of types; perfect citations |
| **Organization** | Is your information organized logically? | Hard to follow; seems random | Clear organization; easy to follow | Excellent flow; transitions; very professional |
| **Analysis** | Do you go beyond facts to interpretation? | Mostly just facts or summary | Some analysis; you interpret information | Strong analysis; you evaluate, compare, synthesize |
| **Presentation** *(format appropriate)* | Is it well-crafted and professional? | Rough draft quality; needs revision | Complete and polished; meets standards | Exceptionally well-crafted; goes above requirements |

---

### Kind Feedback Example:

> **Your Project: Proficient in most areas, Advanced in Research!**
>
> **Glows (What's Working):** 🌟
> - Your research is excellent! You used 6 different credible sources including primary sources. That's advanced-level research!
> - Your organization is clear and easy to follow
>
> **Grows (Next Steps):** 🌱
> - To move your Content Knowledge to Advanced: Go deeper! Instead of just explaining WHAT happened during the event, analyze WHY it happened and what the effects were.
> - To move your Analysis to Advanced: Compare this event to another similar event. What patterns do you see? What's different?
>
> **Suggested Revision:** You can revise and resubmit for a higher score! Focus on adding deeper analysis in sections 2 and 3. Want to meet with me to discuss?
>
> **Overall:** You clearly worked hard on this and learned a lot! I can tell you're proud of it—you should be! 💙

## 📊 Example 6: Single-Point Rubric (Simpler Alternative)

### Assignment: Book Report Essay

**Middle column = the standard. Feedback goes in side columns.**

---

| Areas to Strengthen ⬅️ | **Standard (What I'm Looking For)** | Ways to Exceed ➡️ |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------|------------------|
| | **Introduction:** Clear hook + book title, author + thesis statement about theme | |
| | **Body Paragraphs (3):** Each focuses on one piece of evidence from book that supports thesis | |
| | **Analysis:** You explain HOW each piece of evidence supports your thesis (not just summary!) | |
| | **Conclusion:** Restates thesis + reflects on significance | |
| | **Writing:** Clear sentences, organized paragraphs, few errors | |

---

**How It Works:**
- Middle column describes the learning goal (the standard)
- Left column: Write feedback on what needs work
- Right column: Write feedback on what exceeded expectations
- If student met standard perfectly, no notes needed!

**Example Filled In:**
| Areas to Strengthen ⬅️ | **Standard** | Ways to Exceed ➡️ |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------|------------------|
| Your hook is a question—that works! To make intro stronger, add 1-2 sentences giving context before your thesis. | **Introduction:** Clear hook + book title, author + thesis statement | |
| | **Body Paragraphs:** Each focuses on one piece of evidence from book that supports thesis | Your evidence choices are EXCELLENT—each one really demonstrates the theme in a different way! |
| | **Analysis:** Explain HOW evidence supports thesis | This is your strength! Your analysis goes deep and makes me think! |
| Your conclusion restates thesis. Also add: why does this theme matter? Why should readers care? | **Conclusion:** Restates thesis + reflects on significance | |

## 🎯 Tips for Creating Your Own Values-Aligned Rubrics

### ✅ DO:

1. **Use growth language**
   - "Developing" not "Poor"
   - "Approaching" not "Inadequate"
   - "Mastered" not "Perfect"

2. **Be specific**
   - "Add 3 supporting details" not "improve content"
   - "Check your work in Step 2" not "make corrections"

3. **Show the path forward**
   - Each level explains what to DO to reach next level
   - Student can see exactly what actions will improve their work

4. **Make it a learning tool, not just grading tool**
   - Share before assignment (it's a guide!)
   - Students self-assess during work
   - Use for feedback, not just final grade

5. **Celebrate growth**
   - Note when student has improved from last time
   - Progress matters as much as achievement

---

### ❌ DON'T:

1. **Don't use shame language**
   - Not: "Sloppy," "Careless," "Lazy"
   - Instead: "Let's work on [specific skill]"

2. **Don't be vague**
   - Not: "Good job" or "Needs work"
   - Instead: Specific feedback about specific criteria

3. **Don't make it too complex**
   - 15 criteria with 5 levels each = overwhelming
   - 4-6 key criteria with 3-4 levels = usable

4. **Don't forget the why**
   - Rubric should help students understand what good work looks like
   - If rubric confuses them, simplify it

## 📋 Template: Create Your Own Kind Rubric

```
Assignment: ___________________
Learning Goal: ________________

| Criterion | Still Building | Making Progress | Got It! | Above & Beyond |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|---------|----------------|
| [Key Skill 1] | [What this looks like + kind next step] | [What this looks like + encouragement] | [What this looks like + celebration] | [What this looks like + recognition] |
| [Key Skill 2] | | | | |
| [Key Skill 3] | | | | |

For each level, write:
- What observable evidence shows student is at this level?
- What specific next step would help them improve?
- Use encouraging, growth-focused language!
```

## 💙 Remember:

**A rubric is not a weapon to judge students.**

**A rubric is a map to show students where they are and where they're going.**

When designed with kindness, clarity, and precision, rubrics become powerful learning tools—not sources of anxiety. 🌟
