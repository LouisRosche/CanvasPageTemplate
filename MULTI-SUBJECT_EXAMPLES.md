## 📐 Example 1: High School Geometry

```yaml
CONFIGURATION:
Subject: Mathematics
Grade: 10th Grade
Topic: Properties of Triangles
Duration: Single 90-minute block
Teacher: Ms. Rodriguez
```

### Page Structure:

**🎯 Learning Targets**
- I can identify and classify triangles by sides and angles
- I can prove the Triangle Sum Theorem
- I can apply triangle properties to solve real-world problems
- I can use the Pythagorean Theorem in coordinate geometry

**🔥 Anchor Hook: The Bridge Problem**
> "Engineers need to design a pedestrian bridge with triangular support beams. Why do they always use triangles instead of squares or rectangles? Today you'll discover why triangles are the strongest shape in architecture!"

**📊 Pre-Assessment (5 min)**
- Quick quiz: Triangle basics review
- Identify what you remember from middle school

**Station 1: Triangle Classification Lab (25 min)**
- **🟢 Foundations**: Use manipulatives to sort triangle cards, complete classification chart
- **🟡 Target**: Measure and classify 10 triangles, explain relationships
- **🔴 Extensions**: Create classification tree, find triangles in real architecture photos

**Station 2: Triangle Sum Theorem Investigation (25 min)**
- Use GeoGebra to investigate angle sums
- Record data for 20 different triangles
- Make conjecture and write proof
- **Differentiation**: Scaffolded proof frames vs. independent proof writing

**Station 3: Real-World Applications (25 min)**
- Calculate height of building using similar triangles
- Design strongest bridge truss pattern
- Solve coordinate geometry triangle problems
- **Choice Board**: Choose 2 of 4 application problems

**🎓 Exit Ticket (15 min)**
- Classify mystery triangle from coordinates
- Explain why triangles are rigid structures
- Solve multi-step problem

**📚 Vocabulary**
- Equilateral, Isosceles, Scalene
- Acute, Right, Obtuse
- Congruent, Theorem, Proof

**🔗 Resources**
- GeoGebra interactive tools
- Khan Academy: Triangle properties
- Video: Engineering with triangles
- Formula reference sheet

## 🧪 Example 2: Middle School Science

```yaml
CONFIGURATION:
Subject: Science (Biology)
Grade: 7th Grade
Topic: Cell Structure and Function
Duration: Week-long unit (5 days)
Teacher: Mr. Chen
```

### Page Structure:

**🎯 Learning Targets**
- I can identify the major parts of plant and animal cells
- I can explain the function of each organelle
- I can compare and contrast prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
- I can relate cell structures to their functions

**🔬 Anchor Phenomenon: The Mystery Organism**
> "Scientists discovered a microscopic organism in Yellowstone's hot springs that doesn't fit any known category. It has features of both bacteria AND complex cells. What does this tell us about how life is organized?"

**📋 Day 1: What Do You Know About Cells?**
- KWL chart (Know, Want to know, Learned)
- Misconception identification
- Scale activity (How small is a cell?)

**📖 Day 2: Reading & Research Stations**
- **Station A**: Interactive cell models (digital/physical)
- **Station B**: Reading comprehension - Cell discovery history
- **Station C**: Video analysis - Journey inside the cell
- All students rotate through all stations

**🔬 Day 3: Virtual Lab Investigation**
- Use online microscope simulator
- Compare plant vs. animal cells
- Identify organelles and their locations
- Data collection: Create comparison chart
- **Differentiation**: Labeled diagrams vs. unlabeled identification

**🎨 Day 4: Creative Application**
- **Choice Board**: Select one project
  - Create cell analogy (cell = factory, city, school, etc.)
  - Build 3D cell model
  - Write "diary of an organelle"
  - Create educational video/comic
  - Design cell-themed game

**🧬 Day 5: Assessment & Extension**
- Summative test: Label cells, explain functions, compare types
- Extension: Research current cell biology research
- Reflection: Return to KWL chart, what did you learn?

**📚 Vocabulary** (Expandable sections for each)
- Cell membrane, Nucleus, Mitochondria, Chloroplast
- Cytoplasm, Ribosome, Vacuole, Cell wall
- Prokaryote, Eukaryote, Organelle

**🎥 Video Library**
- Crash Course Biology: Cell structure
- Amoeba Sisters: Plant vs. Animal cells
- TED-Ed: How we see cells

## 📖 Example 3: Elementary ELA

```yaml
CONFIGURATION:
Subject: English Language Arts
Grade: 4th Grade
Topic: Making Inferences from Text
Duration: 3-day mini-unit
Teacher: Mrs. Thompson
```

### Page Structure:

**🎯 I Can Statements**
- I can use clues from the text and my own knowledge to make inferences
- I can find evidence in the text to support my inferences
- I can explain my thinking when I make inferences

**📚 Essential Question**
> "How do good readers figure out things that aren't directly written in a book?"

**🎨 Day 1: Picture Inference Game**
- Look at pictures without captions
- What do you think is happening?
- What clues helped you figure it out?
- **Anchor Chart**: Build "Inference Equation"
  - Text Clues + What I Know = Inference

**📖 Day 2: Reading Detective Work**
- Read short mystery passages
- Circle text clues (mark up the text!)
- Make inferences about: character feelings, setting, what will happen next
- **Graphic Organizer**: Text Clues | What I Know | My Inference
- **Differentiation**:
  - 🟢 Foundations: Teacher reads aloud, guided inference together
  - 🟡 Target: Partner reading, independent inference
  - 🔴 Extensions: More complex texts, multiple inferences

**🎭 Day 3: Inference in Action**
- Read longer passage: "The Empty Playground"
- Make multiple inferences throughout
- Act out what you think happened (drama integration!)
- Write: Explain your inference with evidence
- **Exit Ticket**: New short text, make and defend one inference

**🎮 Interactive Activities**
- Inference picture slideshow
- "Two Truths and an Inference" game
- Reader's theater: Show, don't tell

**📚 Vocabulary**
- Inference, Clues, Evidence
- Text evidence, Prior knowledge
- Context, Conclude

**👨‍👩‍👧 Parent Note**
> "This week we're learning to 'read between the lines.' At home, practice making inferences while reading together. Ask: What do you think? How do you know?"

## 🌍 Example 4: High School Social Studies

```yaml
CONFIGURATION:
Subject: Social Studies (US History)
Grade: 11th Grade
Topic: Causes of the American Revolution
Duration: Two-week unit
Teacher: Mr. Washington
```

### Page Structure:

**🎯 Historical Thinking Skills**
- Analyze multiple primary sources from different perspectives
- Identify causes and consequences of historical events
- Construct historical arguments with evidence
- Evaluate continuity and change over time

**⚡ Driving Question**
> "Why do people risk everything to revolt against authority? When is revolution justified?"

**📜 Week 1: Building Context**

**Day 1-2: The British Perspective**
- Primary sources: British Parliament documents, King George III speeches
- What problems did Britain face after French & Indian War?
- Why did they tax the colonies?
- **Activity**: Role-play Parliament debate

**Day 3-4: The Colonial Perspective**
- Primary sources: Colonial newspapers, protest pamphlets, letters
- What were colonists' grievances?
- How did different groups of colonists respond?
- **Activity**: Create propaganda poster (Patriot OR Loyalist)

**Day 5: Multiple Perspectives**
- Native American perspective
- Enslaved people's perspective
- Women's perspective
- Merchant class perspective
- **Socratic Seminar**: Who benefited from revolution? Who didn't?

**🎯 Week 2: Analysis & Argumentation**

**Day 6-7: Document-Based Question (DBQ) Workshop**
- Analyze 7 primary source documents
- Identify point of view, purpose, audience
- Categorize by type of cause (economic, political, social)
- **Graphic Organizer**: DBQ planning template

**Day 8-9: Essay Writing**
- Thesis workshop: Arguable claims
- Body paragraph structure: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning
- Counterclaim and rebuttal
- **Differentiation**:
  - 🟢 Foundations: Sentence starters, paragraph frames
  - 🟡 Target: Independent writing with rubric
  - 🔴 Extensions: Incorporate historiography (how has interpretation changed?)

**Day 10: Present and Debate**
- Share thesis statements
- Class debate: Were colonists justified?
- Peer feedback on arguments
- Final revision time

**📊 Assessment Components**
- Primary source analysis (ongoing)
- Socratic seminar participation (rubric)
- DBQ essay (summative)
- Self-reflection on historical thinking

**📚 Key Terms** (with historical context)
- Taxation without representation
- Stamp Act, Tea Act, Intolerable Acts
- Boycott, Sons of Liberty, Continental Congress
- Patriot, Loyalist, Neutral

**🔗 Digital Resources**
- Library of Congress primary source collection
- National Archives documents
- Interactive timeline
- Video: Crash Course US History

## 🎵 Example 5: Middle School Music

```yaml
CONFIGURATION:
Subject: Music
Grade: 6th Grade
Topic: Elements of Music - Rhythm and Beat
Duration: 4-week unit
Teacher: Ms. Melody
```

### Page Structure:

**🎯 Musical Skills**
- I can identify and perform steady beat vs. rhythm
- I can read and write basic rhythm notation
- I can create original rhythmic compositions
- I can perform rhythms using body percussion and instruments

**🎵 Essential Question**
> "How do rhythm and beat work together to create music we love?"

**🎧 Week 1: Feel the Beat**

**Listening Station**: Analyze songs from different genres
- Identify the steady beat (clap along)
- Notice the rhythm of the melody (different from beat!)
- Compare: What makes hip-hop rhythm different from classical?

**Movement Station**: Body percussion
- Stomp, clap, snap, pat
- Echo rhythms
- Create 4-beat patterns
- **Video demonstrations** for each technique

**Notation Station**: Introduction to rhythm symbols
- Quarter notes, eighth notes, rests
- Interactive rhythm flashcards
- Practice reading simple patterns
- **Differentiation**: Color-coded notes for support

**🎼 Week 2: Reading Rhythm**

**Practice Station**: Rhythm reading exercises
- Progressive difficulty (1-4 stars)
- Self-paced learning
- Audio examples to check yourself

**Performance Station**: Play rhythms on percussion
- Choose your instrument
- Perform for partner or record video
- Peer feedback using compliment sandwich

**Composition Station**: Create your own 8-beat rhythm
- Use notation software or paper
- Must include: quarter notes, eighth notes, at least one rest
- Test it out by performing

**🎶 Week 3: Rhythm in Context**

**Cultural Exploration**: Rhythms from around the world
- African drumming patterns
- Latin American clave rhythms
- Indian tabla rhythms
- Find connections and differences

**Genre Study**: How does rhythm define music style?
- Rock vs. Jazz vs. Classical vs. Hip-Hop
- Analyze rhythmic features of each
- Try performing in different styles

**🎭 Week 4: Final Performance Project**

**Choose Your Own Adventure**:
1. Solo/duet performance of teacher-provided rhythm piece
2. Original composition with written notation
3. Teach the class a rhythm from another culture
4. Remix popular song with new rhythmic elements

**Rubric Includes**:
- Accuracy of rhythm
- Use of notation
- Performance quality
- Creativity/expression
- Reflection on process

**📱 Digital Tools**
- Soundtrap for composition
- Rhythm trainer apps
- Video recording for self-assessment
- Notation software (Noteflight, MuseScore)

**📚 Music Vocabulary**
- Beat, Rhythm, Tempo
- Quarter note, Eighth note, Rest
- Measure, Time signature
- Syncopation (advanced)

## 🏃 Example 6: Elementary Physical Education

```yaml
CONFIGURATION:
Subject: Physical Education
Grade: 3rd Grade
Topic: Fundamental Movement Skills - Throwing
Duration: 2-week unit (4 PE classes)
Teacher: Coach Martinez
```

### Page Structure:

**🎯 Movement Goals**
- I can demonstrate proper overhand throwing technique
- I can throw with accuracy at different distances
- I can explain how throwing is used in different sports and activities
- I can set goals to improve my throwing skills

**⚾ Why Learn This?**
> "Throwing is a skill you'll use in sports, games, and everyday life! Let's become throwing experts!"

**🏃 Day 1: Throw Like a Pro**

**Warm-Up** (5 min): Dynamic stretching with music

**Teach & Model** (10 min):
- Teacher demonstration
- Student helpers show good/bad examples
- Poster: 5 Steps of Perfect Throw
  1. Side to target
  2. Ball in hand behind ear
  3. Step with opposite foot
  4. Rotate hips
  5. Follow through

**Skill Stations** (25 min): Rotate every 5 minutes
1. **Wall Target Practice**: Throw at bullseye (accuracy)
2. **Distance Challenge**: See how far you can throw
3. **Partner Toss**: Practice with feedback
4. **Video Analysis**: Record yourself, watch in slow motion
5. **Game Application**: Modified throwing game

**Cool Down & Reflection** (5 min):
- Stretching
- Thumbs up/middle/down: How did you do today?

**🎯 Day 2: Accuracy Challenge**

**Warm-Up Game** (5 min): Catch the tail

**Skill Review** (5 min): Quick review of 5 steps

**Station Rotations** (30 min):
- Each station focuses on accuracy at different distances
- Self-assessment checklist at each station
- **Differentiation**: Different distance markers for different skill levels
  - 🟢 Closer targets, bigger landing zones
  - 🟡 Standard distance
  - 🔴 Longer distance, smaller targets

**Cool Down** (5 min): Partner stretches, goal setting

**⚡ Day 3: Throwing Games**

**Warm-Up** (5 min): Throw & Catch relay

**Game Stations** (35 min): Apply throwing in game contexts
1. **Knockout**: Throw to knock down pins
2. **Target Points**: Different targets worth different points
3. **Long Throw Contest**: Distance competition
4. **Partner Rally**: Keep ball going back and forth

**Reflection** (5 min):
- Which game helped you practice most?
- What did you do well?
- What will you work on next time?

**🏆 Day 4: Skills Test & Celebration**

**Warm-Up** (5 min): Student-led

**Skills Assessment** (20 min):
- Technique checklist (teacher observes)
- Accuracy test (3 throws at target, record score)
- Distance test (best of 3 throws)
- **Self-Assessment**: Compare to Day 1 goals

**Celebration Game** (20 min):
- Class favorite throwing game (voted on)
- Everyone participates at their level

**Reflection & Goal Setting** (5 min):
- What improved?
- Set goal for next unit (catching!)

**📊 Progress Tracking**
- Photo/video portfolio of technique improvement
- Personal best chart (accuracy & distance)
- Reflection journal (digital or paper)

**🏠 Family Connection**
> "Practice throwing at home! Play catch with family, throw ball at target on garage, or practice throwing into laundry basket!"

**♿ Modifications & Adaptations**
- Different sized balls (tennis ball, foam ball, beach ball)
- Shorter distances
- Seated throwing
- Balloon toss for students with limited mobility
- Partner assistance

## 🔬 Example 7: High School Chemistry

```yaml
CONFIGURATION:
Subject: Science (Chemistry)
Grade: 11th Grade (Honors)
Topic: Chemical Equilibrium
Duration: 10-day unit
Teacher: Dr. Reeves
```

### Page Structure:

**🎯 Learning Targets**
- I can explain what chemical equilibrium means at the molecular level
- I can use Le Chatelier's Principle to predict how systems respond to stress
- I can calculate equilibrium constants (Keq) from concentration data
- I can design experiments to shift equilibrium and explain results

**⚗️ Driving Question**
> "Why doesn't all the CO₂ in a soda bottle escape when you open it? How do our bodies maintain perfect pH despite what we eat? The answer: Chemical Equilibrium!"

**📅 Unit Overview**

**Days 1-2: What is Equilibrium?**

**Simulation Investigation**:
- PhET: Reversible Reactions simulation
- Observe forward and reverse reactions
- Graph concentrations over time
- Discover: Equilibrium ≠ Equal amounts!

**Conceptual Understanding**:
- Particle diagrams of equilibrium systems
- Common misconceptions addressed
- Real-world examples: Coca-Cola, blood pH, Haber process

**Days 3-4: Le Chatelier's Principle**

**Demonstration Lab** (Teacher-led):
- Cobalt chloride equilibrium (color change)
- Manipulate: Temperature, concentration, pressure
- Class predictions before each change
- Discuss: Why did system shift?

**Practice Problems**:
- Predict equilibrium shifts
- Explain using collision theory
- **Differentiation**:
  - 🟢 Foundations: Guided notes, structured practice
  - 🟡 Target: Independent problems with self-check
  - 🔴 Extensions: Complex scenarios, multiple stresses

**Days 5-6: Equilibrium Calculations**

**Math Workshop**:
- Introduction to Keq expression
- ICE tables (Initial, Change, Equilibrium)
- Step-by-step calculation practice
- **Scaffolding**: Worked examples → Guided practice → Independent

**Problem Sets**:
- Level 1: Calculate Keq given equilibrium concentrations
- Level 2: Calculate equilibrium concentrations given Keq
- Level 3: Find Keq from initial concentrations and percent dissociation
- Level 4: Challenge problems (AP-style)

**Days 7-8: Lab Investigation**

**Student-Designed Experiment**:
- Question: How does [factor] affect equilibrium position?
- Design procedure to test
- Collect quantitative or qualitative data
- Analysis: Use Le Chatelier's to explain results

**Lab Report Components**:
- Hypothesis with reasoning
- Procedure with safety considerations
- Data tables and graphs
- Analysis and conclusion
- Error analysis

**Days 9-10: Synthesis & Assessment**

**Case Study**: Industrial Haber Process
- Read about ammonia production
- Economic and environmental considerations
- How do chemists optimize yield?
- Connection: Equilibrium principles in action

**Summative Assessment**:
- Part 1: Conceptual questions (explain equilibrium shifts)
- Part 2: Calculations (Keq and ICE tables)
- Part 3: Lab practical (unknown system, predict and test)
- Part 4: Written response (apply to real-world scenario)

**📚 Vocabulary & Concepts**
- Chemical equilibrium, Reversible reaction
- Le Chatelier's Principle, Stress
- Equilibrium constant (Keq), Reaction quotient (Q)
- ICE table, Dynamic equilibrium

**🔗 Resources**
- PhET simulations
- Khan Academy: Equilibrium calculations
- Practice problem generator
- Virtual lab tools
- AP Chemistry resources

**🏠 Homework & Extensions**
- Daily practice problems (graded for completion)
- Video lessons (flipped classroom option)
- AP-style FRQ practice
- Research: Equilibrium in biological systems

## 🎨 Key Takeaways

### What Makes This Universal?

1. **Same Core Structure**
   - Learning targets clearly stated
   - Engaging hook/phenomenon
   - Multiple learning activities
   - Formative and summative assessment
   - Reflection built in

2. **Adaptable Components**
   - Vocabulary adjusts to discipline
   - Activities match subject needs
   - Assessment types vary by content
   - Resources tailored to topic

3. **Consistent Student Experience**
   - Always know what to expect
   - Same navigation pattern
   - Familiar layout across subjects
   - Reduces cognitive load

4. **Built-In Best Practices**
   - Differentiation options
   - Multiple representations (UDL)
   - Active learning emphasized
   - Real-world connections

5. **Teacher Efficiency**
   - Reusable template structure
   - Easy customization
   - Professional design
   - Time-saving features
